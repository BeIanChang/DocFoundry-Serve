Question: Can unused vacation days carry over into the next calendar year?

Sources:
[D1] Employee Handbook 2025
[S1] Unused vacation days do not automatically carry over into the next calendar year. Employees are expected to schedule and use accrued vacation by December 31.
[S2] Up to 5 unused vacation days may be carried over only with written manager approval submitted before year end.
[D2] Benefits FAQ
[S3] Not automatically. Most employees are expected to use accrued vacation by the end of the calendar year. Limited carryover may be approved through the normal process.

Context:
[S1] Unused vacation days do not automatically carry over into the next calendar year. Employees are expected to schedule and use accrued vacation by December 31.

Question: When is HR approval required for PTO carryover?

Sources:
[D1] Employee Handbook 2025
[S1] Carryover requests above 5 days require Human Resources approval and are limited to exceptional business continuity situations, such as major incidents, staffing shortages during critical launches, or company-directed blackout periods.
[D2] HR Policy Exceptions Memo
[S2] Requests above 5 days require both manager approval and HR approval.
[S3] HR review is appropriate only when the employee could not reasonably take leave because of documented business continuity needs, a formal leave coordination issue, or an organization-wide restriction on time off.

[S2] Requests above 5 days require both manager approval and HR approval.

[S3] HR review is appropriate only when the employee could not reasonably take leave because of documented business continuity needs, a formal leave coordination issue, or an organization-wide restriction on time off.

Question: What happens if someone submits a carryover request after December 31?

Sources:
[D1] Employee Handbook 2025
[S1] Carryover requests submitted after December 31 are generally not approved unless a separately documented exception applies.
[D2] HR Policy Exceptions Memo
[S2] If a request is submitted after the deadline, the default outcome is denial.
[S3] HR may review late requests only when the delay was caused by a system outage, emergency leave event, or a documented instruction from company leadership to defer time off.
[D3] Benefits FAQ
[S4] Late requests are normally denied, but HR may review them if there was a documented emergency, system outage, or company-directed restriction that prevented timely submission.

Context:
[S1] Carryover requests submitted after December 31 are generally not approved unless a separately documented exception applies.

[S2] If a request is submitted after the deadline, the default outcome is denial.

[S3] HR may review late requests only when the delay was caused by a system outage, emergency leave event, or a documented instruction from company leadership to defer time off.

[S4] Late requests are normally denied, but HR may review them if there was a documented emergency, system outage, or company-directed restriction that prevented timely submission.
